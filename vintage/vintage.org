* OFFICIAL SOLUTION
  there's no guided mode, so let's take a look at the official solution; turns
  out, there's a machine called =FS01= we can identify from the bloodhound data
  is a member of the =Pre-Windows 2000 Compatible Access= Group. They don't
  explain how they got there other than by checking users and computers data
  from the injestor;
* ippsec
  talks about cypher queries and how nothing immediately stands out; checks
  the =shortest path to tier zero= query and notices the =cneri_adm= and
  =lbianchi_adm= users who can add themselves to the =DELEGATEDADMINS= group; this
  is probably used for some sort of RBCD attack; so set =DELEGATEDADMINS= as the
  starting node and the DC as the end node and it turns out they have an
  =AllowedToAct= over the DC. also notes that the =_adm= accounts usually have a
  password reuse issue where the normal users reuse their passwords for the
  =_adm= accounts; adds both =c.neri= and =l.bianchi= to *HIGH VALUE* on bloodhound;

** the gMSA01$ account
   examining this on bloodhound, we see that this account has the =AddSelf= and
   =GenericWrite= over =ServiceManagers=; also, the =DOMAINCOMPUTERS= group has the
   =ReadGMSAPassword= right over this account; the only member of the
   =DOMAINCOMPUTERS= is the =FS01= account; looking at what it is a member of, we
   see the =Pre-Windows 2000 Compatible Access= Group
   turns out, as noted by the [[https://trustedsec.com/blog/diving-into-pre-created-computer-accounts][TrustedSec blog]], on creating computer accounts
   with the *"Assign this computer account as a pre-Windows 2000 computer"*
   checkmark, the password for the computer account becomes the same as the
   computer account in lowercase.
